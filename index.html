Technology

Fiber Loop Photonics — a 4‑step optical pipeline

ALTA replaces heavy, power-hungry electronic subsystems with a continuous optical path from sensor to downlink. No EMI. Nanosecond decisions. Mass and watts saved by orders of magnitude.

01

Sensing

Fiber Bragg Gratings (FBG)

Rad-hard, passive optical sensors monitor thermal, vibration, and radiation events across the bus.

TRL 9 ESA / NASA qualified
Look inside

How it works

Sense in light. Across radiation. Without electronics.

A Bragg grating is a periodic refractive-index pattern written into a fiber's core. It reflects one specific wavelength — λBragg — and passes the rest. As heat, strain, or radiation perturbs the fiber, λBragg shifts by a known coefficient. The sensor is the fiber itself: passive, distributed, rad-hard by physics.

ESA-qualified EMI-immune 50-year heritage

Why this mattersOne sensor type, multiplexed along a single fiber, can replace dozens of vulnerable electronic sensors — and survives radiation environments where silicon stalls.

02

Processing

Photonic Reservoir Computing

All-optical pattern and anomaly detection at light speed. Energy scales as O(1) instead of O(N²) for digital counterparts (Wang et al., 2025).

TRL 4–5 Sub-linear power scaling
Look inside

How it works

A loop of light, thinking in nanoseconds.

A short pulse enters a fiber loop with one nonlinear element. As it circulates, the loop's delay maps onto a sequence of virtual neurons — a high-dimensional reservoir state. The readout layer projects this to a decision. Inference happens at the speed of light through the loop; only the readout weights are trained.

O(1) energy Picosecond inference No backprop

Why this mattersEnergy per decision is constant regardless of reservoir size — the same loop scales from 100 to 10,000 virtual neurons without burning a single extra watt (Wang et al., 2025).

03

Internal Comms

Fiber-Optic Backbone

A single fiber replaces ~100 copper wires for power and attitude coordination across the platform.

TRL 6 100 Gbps · 10× SpaceFibre
Look inside

How it works

One hundred wires become one fiber.

Conventional spacecraft buses run hundreds of copper conductors between subsystems — heavy, noisy, EMI-coupled. A single multi-mode fiber with wavelength-division multiplexing carries all of it at 100 Gbps, ten times the SpaceFibre standard. Different subsystems travel on different wavelengths through the same physical strand.

100 Gbps 60% mass cut Zero EMI

Why this mattersThe fiber backbone is the platform's nervous system: lighter, faster, electrically silent — and a smaller attack surface for radiation-induced upsets.

External Link

Laser Terminal

Decisions — not raw data — are downlinked. 95% on-board volume reduction means more science per pass.

TRL 6–7 10 Gbps optical downlink
Look inside

How it works

Decisions, not data.

Conventional missions downlink raw telemetry and process on the ground. ALTA's pipeline already decides on-board, so the laser terminal sends only compressed decisions and event summaries — 95% of raw data volume is eliminated before transmission.

10 Gbps TESAT-class heritage 95% on-board reduction

Why this mattersMore science per pass. Less ground latency. A constellation that thinks for itself instead of phoning home for every decision.

Primary use case

Real-time autonomous anomaly response — e.g. immediate thermal-runaway detection triggering autonomous venting, with no ground in the loop.

  1. FBG
    Sense
    — ns

    Fiber Bragg gratings register a wavelength shift in the affected zone. The sensor line is passive optics — no electronics in the loop, no EMI to fight.

  2. PRC
    Infer
    — ns

    The photonic reservoir computes the signature. Light circulates the fiber loop; the readout layer classifies it as thermal-runaway with high confidence.

  3. ↳
    Decide
    — ns

    Decision logic dispatches the vent command on the photonic backbone. No ground-station round-trip; the platform decides locally.

  4. VENT
    Act
    — μs

    The vent valve actuates. End-to-end, from FBG shift to mechanical response, well under a microsecond after the event.
